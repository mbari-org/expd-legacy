How depth is calculated for
MBARI ROV CTD data

Pressure is the measured value
Pressure is measured by sensors on oceanographic instruments, such as CTD packages (conductivity/temperature/depth). For Ventana and Tiburon ROV CTD pressure, the Falmouth Scientific sensor precision is about 1 dbar and the data is archived to 0.01 dbar in the raw data files, "derived data" files, and in the expedition database.

Depth is derived from pressure
Depth is often calculated from pressure because depth is more commonly used by scientists to estimate distance below the sea surface and interpret other data. A variety of formulas may be used. For the Ventana and Tiburon ROV CTD depth calculations, the Fofonoff and Millard (1982) algorithm is applied during processing. The depth values are archived in the ROV CTD "derived data" files and in the expedition database. Because of the assumptions and mathematical contortions involved, those who require precise knowledge of ocean parameters find it is better to work directly with the pressure data.

The algorithm
The algorithm used to calculate MBARI ROV CTD depth, in meters, from pressure, in decibars (dbar), is Fofonoff and Millard (1982) with sea level pressure = 0 dbar. This formula incorporates an equation of state of seawater and the hydrostatic equation, which includes variation of gravity with latitude and depth, and is computed with a least squares polynomial fit. It is reported to be accurate to 0.1 m over the full ocean pressure range of 0 to 10,000 dbar; some sources of error are discussed below. It is endorsed by the UNESCO/SCOR/ICES/IAPSO Joint Panel on Oceanographic Tables and Standards and SCOR Working Group 51 on evaluation of CTD data. Click here to see our depth calculation code. At MBARI, an older algorithm used to be employed to calculate depth (Saunders and Fofonoff, 1976), but the newer algorithm now has been applied to all of our old ROV CTD pressure data to derive depth.

Atmospheric pressure correction
The measured pressure is that of the overlying water plus the atmospheric pressure above the surface, which is 1 atm (10 dbar), more or less, at sea level. By convention, the atmospheric pressure component is removed so it is zero at the sea surface. To do this for the ROV CTD data, a constant offset of 10 dbar is subtracted from the initial measurement before pressure is logged, so a slight error is introduced if actual atmospheric pressure is different.

Equation of state of seawater
The equation of state of seawater used in this algorithm is the Knudsen-Ekman equation of state. It is for a "standard ocean" of T=0oC and S=35‰, so it makes some assumptions about the density distributions in the water column that are not corrected for actual conditions.

Variation with latitude
The force of gravity varies slightly with latitude due to the earth's geoid. As a result, 10,000 decibars pressure converts to 9725.47 m at the equator, 9699.84 m at 45o latitude, and 9674.23 m at the poles (Fofonoff and Millard, 1982). For MBARI ROV CTD depth calculations, a constant latitude is supplied to the equation. For data acquired after 8/1/1999, this is the ship's median latitude for the expedition, and if the ship traverses more than 0.1 decimal degree latitude during the expedition, the median latitude is recalculated for the data that fall inside that new 0.1o. For expeditions prior to 8/1/1999, this same method was used, but for cruises where there was no navigation data, a value of 36.75o latitude (middle of Monterey Bay) was applied.

ROV vehicle depth clarification
The ROVs have a second pressure sensor on board (Paroscientific, http://www.paroscientific.com/Ds.htm), which is separate from the CTD package and whose pressure data are archived in the ROV navigation data files rather than the CTD data files. From this pressure data, depth is calculated in the vehicle software with a different algorithm that makes no correction for the equation of state of seawater and uses a different, manually supplied constant for latitude. These depth values are the vehicle depth displayed on the ROV console video overlay, are used by Winfrog and ArcView, but are not archived in the navigation data files.
